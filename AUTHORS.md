this note. A contribution to the underlying finding is not the same as agreeing to put
your name on a claim that generalises it — the whole subject of the note is values
published without the conditions that produced them, and an assumed author is one.

## To confirm or decline

Open a PR against this file, or say so in an issue. Declining costs nothing and is a
perfectly good outcome — so is "the finding is mine, the generalisation is not".

If §4 misrepresents the composition work, correcting it matters more than the note does.

**Already happened twice.** The first draft filed composition as a third arrival at one
rule. Its author read it and said the objection we had raised against ourselves was the
right one to keep — so the note now has two conditions and a boundary case, which is a
smaller and truer claim than the one it started with.

The second: the draft separated conditions from relations by intuition and had no test.
babyblueviper1 supplied one — *free byproduct of the same observation, or a separate
computation that could have been skipped?* — which is now §6 and is the thing the note
stands or falls on. He also brought a fourth arrival independently, before reading any of
this.

**Anyone listed here may remove themselves at any time**, and a name in this table is a
claim about agreement, not about contribution.
